In August we took a chill weekend camping trip up to the
[Mt. Baker Snoqualmie National Forest](https://www.nationalforests.org/forest/mt-baker-snoqualmie-national-forest).
With a planned weekend of doing very little I grabbed a couple radio bags and
added them to the camp gear, you know, just in case.

## Antenna

For this outing I only took the trusty Chameleon tactical delta loop
([CHA TDL](https://chameleonantenna.com/products/tactical-delta-loop-tdl))
mounted on the rock-solid Bosch
[BT160](https://www.boschtools.com/us/en/products/bt160-0601091210) contractors
tripod using the Chameleon
([CHA SURVEYOR-A](https://chameleonantenna.com/products/cha-surveyor-a-surveyor-tripod-adapter)).
I also used two 27" extensions
([CHA SINGLE-EXT](https://chameleonantenna.com/products/cha-single-ext-pack-of-2))
on top of the tripod to raise the loop as high as I could. I connected this to
the radio with 25' of choked RG-316
([CHA RG-316](https://chameleonantenna.com/products/cha-rg-316?variant=46594486927606)).

The final results can be seen here.

![Chameleon CHA-TDL](/assets/img/posts/ham-camping-mtbaker-chatdl.jpeg)

## Radio

The radio I took was my go-to camping HF rig, the Lab599
[TX-500](https://lab599.com/products/tproduct/506522908-147130382471-tx-500-discovery).
The main reason for liking this for camping is that it's so rugged, it can deal
with the dust of a summer site and the occasional rain of an August outing in
the mountains. I keep the radio and a bunch of accessories in a really the
really nice [large gear case](https://nestout.com/products/gear-case-large) by
Nestout, which also makes a handy flat surface as you can see.

![Lab599 TX-500](/assets/img/posts/ham-camping-mtbaker-lab599-tx500.jpeg)

I did buy the [BP-550](http://lab599.com/products/tproduct/957371911-275215165371-bp-550-smart-battery-pack)
battery box which I'm using here, so no need for any power cables.

## Results

Not good. The 6m band was closed all weekend, and while I did hear some really
weak signals on 10m I couldn't scare up a response to any CQ. On the other hand,
it was intended as a chill weekend and I got to practice putting this antenna
up again and watched it deal with some wind too. I spent some more time with the
TX-500 manual as well and some of the less used menu settings so it really was
a nice weekend.
